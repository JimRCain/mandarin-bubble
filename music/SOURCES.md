# Music sources and reproduction

Everything here is generated by `tools/`. The MIDI is the source of truth; the
`.ogg` files are build artifacts. Nothing is hand-edited.

## Rebuild

    python3 tools/compose.py    # writes *.mid + stems/*.{dsk,gm}.mid
    python3 tools/render.py     # render, trim to the barline, encode *.ogg + preview/*.mp3
    python3 tools/ab_gm.py      # renders the GM-emulation A/B for comparison

Requires: `mido`, `numpy`, `fluidsynth`, `ffmpeg`.

### Licensing: what we may and may not ship

The two audio routes have different legal footprints, and this decides them:

* **Pre-rendered OGG (current) — clear.** The `.ogg` files are music *made with*
  the instrument. A virtual-instrument use grant covers distributing the music,
  which is the instrument's whole purpose. We ship no samples and no font, so the
  font's silence on redistribution never comes into play.
* **MIDI + soundfont — unblocked 2026-10-05.** Shipping the font meant
  redistributing the instrument itself, which DSK's bare use grant did not cover;
  Victor Castilla granted it in writing for a reduced subset of the five melodic
  presets (`soundfonts/PERMISSION-DSK.md`). Commit the subset only, never the
  11.5 MB original, and carry the credit line and link. A permissively-licensed
  Chinese font would still be the cleaner option, and a search did **not** turn
  one up; Freesound's erhu/guzheng "CC0" leads are uploads *of* free VSTs, so
  their CC tag cannot launder the underlying samples.

So the clean fonts have no 民乐 and the 民乐 font is the one with no licence. That
is the whole problem, and it only affects the MIDI route.

### If we ever ship a font

Permission is in hand (`soundfonts/PERMISSION-DSK.md`). Downsampling is the lever
that makes it viable — phone speakers cannot resolve
44.1 kHz, so a subset of the five used presets at ~16–22 kHz mono should land in
the low single-digit MB. **That figure is an estimate, not a measurement:** the
SF2 generator tables would not yield per-preset sample totals through our parser
(the sampleID generator never resolved to valid indices), so build and weigh a
real subset before quoting a number.

DSK bank 0 presets: PIPA 0 · PIPA TREM 1 · LUAN 2 · GUZHEN 3 · ERHU 4 · BAN-DI 5 ·
Chinese Percussion 6.

### Three things that will bite you

1. **DSK percussion works, but only on 13 notes: MIDI 54–70** (bank 0 prog 6).
   Probe **one note per render** — on a fixed note grid a long decay bleeds between
   steps and mislabels the map. Notes 84–96 are unmapped.
2. **Soundfonts CAN be layered in one pass — the LAST loaded font wins** a contested
   preset (corr 1.000 vs the winner, −0.139 vs the loser, both load orders). So
   `[FluidR3_GM, DSK]`: DSK overrides progs 0–6, GM supplies prog 32 (bass).
3. **Percussion outlives the barline.** FluidSynth pads the render ~30 s past the last
   note (tail RMS 0.0007 — silence, not ring), so a 16-bar loop measures 74–90 s.
   **Trim to `bars*4*60/bpm` seconds** or the loop will never line up.

## The songs

| song | bpm | loop | MIDI | OGG | feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `jinghong-dawn` | 92 | 41.7 s | 2,378 B | 214 KB | warm, morning |
| `lanterns` | 76 | 50.5 s | 2,388 B | 263 KB | brighter, more rhythmic |
| `mist-on-the-river` | 66 | 58.2 s | 2,285 B | 294 KB | slow, sparse, contemplative |

All in C pentatonic (宫商角徵羽). Layers: guzheng (texture) · pipa (pulse) · bass ·
percussion · erhu (lead) · ban-di (counter) · pipa-trem (intensity), with
staggered entries at bars 1 / 5 / 9 / 13 — so any song can be mixed calm, normal
or intense without recomposing it.
